A Note from Your Neighbor

WWe share a small, quiet loop of street — the kind of place where you know which dog belongs to which porch and whose recycling bin always blows over. What we don't quite have yet is the tradition of getting everyone in one spot at the same time, on purpose.

So this is the start of one. The first Little York Block Party will be Saturday, August 1, 2026 — and if it goes the way we hope, the first Saturday of every August from here on out. We're asking the township about closing off our stretch of the road for the afternoon. We'll have tables, music, games for the kids, and as much food and drink as we collectively decide to bring.

Fill out the form below to tell us you're in, what you might bring, and whether you've got an idea that'd make this even better. The more of us who say yes, the better the day gets.

— Your neighbors at 7643 Kitner

About Little York

LLong before the loop and the cul-de-sacs, this little corner of Northfield Center Township was a community in its own right. George Leach settled in Little York in 1832, shipping lumber out on the newly-completed Ohio-Erie Canal. Brandywine Creek — much wider then than it is today — was joined here by Indian Run, draining the land that became Macedonia.

New Englanders from Vermont, New Hampshire, and Massachusetts brought their town-meeting traditions west with them. A bridge over the Brandywine, a few homesteads, a cheese factory, a church on the green — that was the whole place.

We're not building anything that grand. Just continuing a thing that's been going on around here, in one form or another, for almost two hundred years: neighbors getting together to share a meal and figure out who lives next door.
