a bit of neighborhood trivia

About our corner of Northfield

Our three streets are a midcentury slice of a much older story. Long before Kitner, Shady, and Oviatt were platted in the late 1950s, this corner of Northfield Center was already a community. George Leach settled nearby in Little York in 1832, shipping lumber out on the newly-completed Ohio-Erie Canal. The Brandywine Creek, much wider then than it is today, was joined here by Indian Run, draining the land that became Macedonia.

New Englanders from Vermont, New Hampshire, and Massachusetts brought their town-meeting traditions west with them. A bridge over the Brandywine. A few homesteads. A cheese factory. A church on the green. Now there's us. Three quiet streets off Olde Eight.

We're not building anything that grand. Just continuing a thing that's been going on around here, in one form or another, for almost two hundred years: neighbors getting together to share a meal and figure out who lives next door.

Where it'll happen

We'll close the street between the two ✕ marks.

We've coordinated with Northfield Center Township to close this stretch of the loop for the afternoon. One lane stays clear so emergency vehicles can pass through and hydrants stay accessible. Tables and chairs go on one side only.
